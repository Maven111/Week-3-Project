# Buy by Barter
## Product Journal

This journal documents the development of Buy by Barter, including product decisions, changes, experiments, and updates made during development.

---

# Project Start

I started the project by building the first version of the Buy by Barter app.

The first version focused on bringing the core barter idea to life and creating the foundation for users to discover items and exchange products they already own for things they want.

After building the first version, I invited Git into the project environment to track changes, manage versions, and keep a record of the development process.

---

# Product Direction

The core idea behind Buy by Barter is simple:

> Trade what you have for what you want.

The platform allows users to discover products, barter items they already own, and keep track of their listings and barter history.

The product is designed to be Nigeria-centric, with Nigerian names, locations, currency, products, and everyday references.

---

# Marketplace Updates

## Changed "List an Item" to "Barter an Item"

The main call-to-action was changed from:

> List an item

to:

> Barter an item

The change better reflects the purpose of the platform.

Buy by Barter is not intended to feel like a normal classifieds marketplace where people simply list products for sale.

The action should communicate that the user is putting an item up for exchange.

---

# Category Updates

## Home & Tools

Furniture listings and other random home items were added to the Home & Tools section.

The goal is to make the category feel more useful and representative of the kinds of things people may actually have around their homes that could be exchanged.

The Sport and Outdoor section was removed.

Items that were previously placed under Sport and Outdoor were moved into Home & Tools.

---

## Vintage & Fashion

More old and vintage products were added to this section.

These include old phones and other vintage and Nigeria-centric items.

The goal is to make the section feel less generic and more connected to items people may recognise from Nigerian homes and everyday life.

# Nigeria-Centric Product Experience

The product was updated to feel more relevant to Nigerian users.

This includes:

- Nigerian currency
- Nigerian names
- Nigerian locations
- Nigerian-centric products
- Nigerian vintage items
- Local neighbourhood references

The goal is for the marketplace to feel like a product built for the Nigerian market rather than a generic global marketplace with Nigerian currency added later.

---

# Location-Based Discovery

An interactive location filter was added to help users find items based on proximity.

Users can filter listings by neighbourhood distance.

For example:

> Within 5 miles

This is intended to make it easier for users to find items that are close enough for a physical barter exchange.

Location is particularly important because Buy by Barter involves physical products.

A useful barter match is not only about finding the right product.

It can also depend on how close the other user is.

---

# Bookmarking

A bookmarking feature was added.

Users can bookmark items they want to find quickly later.

This allows users to save interesting listings without immediately making a barter offer.

The feature supports a common browsing behaviour:

> "I like this. Let me save it and come back to it later."

---

# Backup and Restore

The product was updated to allow users to back up and restore their closet listings and barter history as a `.json` file.

Users can export their data and restore it later.

The backup includes relevant information about:

- Closet listings
- Barter history

This gives users more control over their own marketplace data.

---

# Light and Dark Mode

Both light mode and dark mode are supported.

Light mode is the default.

The goal is to give users a choice while keeping the first experience clean and familiar.

# Current Product Thinking

The project is moving beyond simply creating a marketplace interface.

The broader direction is to make Buy by Barter a product that helps people:

1. Discover valuable items.
2. Understand what they can get with what they already own.
3. Find relevant barter opportunities.
4. Make exchanges easier.
5. Keep track of the things they have listed and exchanged.
6. Interact with the brand through useful and entertaining experiences.

The core product remains the barter marketplace.

The additional ideas around Shopping IQ, advertising, events, delivery, PR boxes, installations, and branded physical experiences are opportunities to make the product more engaging and visible as the project develops.

---

# Questions to Explore

As development continues, the following questions need to be answered:

- Will people actually prefer bartering to selling?
- Which product categories generate the most barter activity?
- How useful is neighbourhood-based matching?
- How often will users make multi-item offers?
- How important is estimated item value?
- Will users use bookmarks regularly?
- Does Shopping IQ increase engagement?
- What type of reward would make Shopping IQ meaningful?
- How could real-time product advertising work in practice?
- When should delivery become part of the product?
- What level of trust and verification is required for physical exchanges?
- How can the platform solve the problem of finding a suitable barter match?

These questions will guide future product decisions.

---

# Product Principle

The central idea remains:

> **People already own value. Buy by Barter helps them exchange that value for something they actually want.**
